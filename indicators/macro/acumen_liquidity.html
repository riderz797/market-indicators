Construction (Howell Methodology)
1CB Liquidity = Fed (WALCL − TGA − RRP) + ECB + BoJ + China FX
2Private Liquidity = US Bank Credit (H.8) + Eurodollar Leg (NDFA, net due to foreign offices)
3Gross Global Liquidity = CB + Private
4Drains: −3% per +$10/bbl oil • −4% per +10 MOVE pts • −10% per +10% DXY • −3% per −10bp bills vs the RRP floor (vs 2yr baseline, capped ±30%)
5Net Liquidity = Gross × (1 + drain %)
6Indicator = 3-month MA of YoY % change in Net Liquidity

Both indicator and benchmark are converted to z-scores (2015–present mean/std) before plotting. The liquidity series is then time-shifted 200 days forward to show its predictive signal for equities.

Note on the collateral drain. Howell publishes coefficients for oil, the MOVE index and the dollar; he does not publish one for collateral. The −3%-per-10bp figure is a judgment call sized to sit alongside the existing terms, not a fitted parameter. Adding the eurodollar leg and this drain moved the optimal lead from 190 to 200 days and the full-sample correlation from 0.749 to 0.754 (post-2020, 0.794 to 0.809) — a marginal gain, within noise, and attributable almost entirely to the collateral term. The eurodollar leg is retained because a “global” liquidity measure whose private component counted only onshore bank credit was conceptually incomplete, not because it improves the fit.
