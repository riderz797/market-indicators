Snider's Signal BoardMONETARY

Reads monetary conditions off market signals rather than macroeconomic aggregates, following Jeff Snider's Eurodollar University framework. The premise: since the eurodollar system went off-ledger in the 1960s the money supply is unmeasurable, so conditions have to be reconstructed from the prices that embed them. The goal is not to time a top — it is to eliminate investment quadrants the market has already ruled out.

Anchor spread — the discriminator
5y5y forward minus 5y spot. Deeply negative means the front end is carrying a shock the forward refuses to confirm — a supply shock, not an inflation regime.
What this board answers

Is inflation a regime change, or a front-loaded shock passing through?

A breakeven is not a forecast of consumer prices. It is the market's relative demand for inflation protection — what people will pay to be insured. The payload is the term structure, not the level.

In 2021–22 the 5-year breakeven went to 3.59% while the 5y5y forward sat at 2.31%. The market was pricing a shock passing through, not a new regime. A forward that moves on its own is what a genuine regime change looks like.

How the score is built

Deviation — forward vs its own expanding-median anchor (not a rolling window, which re-bases after a crash and inverts the signal).

Momentum — 26-week change in the forward.

Shock — the anchor spread, floored at zero. A negative spread is evidence of a front-loaded shock; a positive one usually means the front end has collapsed in a deflation scare (November 2008 printed −1.94% at 5 years), so it is never allowed to argue for inflation.

Sources: T5YIE T10YIE T5YIFR DGS30 DFII30 — daily from 2003, resampled weekly.

Standing in for a signal that no longer ships. Snider's primary indicator is the interest-rate swap spread, which bundles forward rate expectations, dealer balance-sheet capacity, demand for duration and collateral conditions into one price. FRED's swap series ended 2016-10-28 and live swap rates are licence-restricted, so this board reconstructs the same underlying information from official sources. It is a proxy, and it cannot reach 2008 — no modern funding series can.

Why these five

Each captures one dimension a swap spread would carry. All are signed so that positive means tighter.

The bills-versus-floor component is inverted deliberately: bills trading below the reverse-repo award rate is the stress state, because it means collateral is scarce enough that buyers accept a yield worse than the risk-free floor.

Net due to related foreign offices
US banks' net dollar funding position with their own offshore offices, in billions. The eurodollar system's visible footprint on a domestic release.
Eurodollar composite
Offshore funding growth against dollar strength, both as z-scores. Below zero is contraction — a rising dollar is eurodollar scarcity in this framework, not a separate fact about currencies.
Why this series

There is no measure of the eurodollar system. NDFACBW027SBOG is the closest free proxy: the H.8 line for US banks' net position with their own foreign offices, weekly and current, starting June 2004 — so unlike Board 2 it covers August 2007.

It read −1.8 in the week of 9 August 2007, the day BNP Paribas froze its funds, and bottomed near −2.2 the week Lehman failed.

A measurement note

This is a net position: it crosses zero 48 times across the sample and runs from −$165bn to +$750bn. A year-over-year percent change on it is meaningless — it exceeds 70,000% either side of a zero crossing.

The board therefore uses the 52-week level change in billions, which is well defined across sign changes, with the rolling z-score absorbing the growth of the banking system over time.

Treasury International Capital was evaluated as a backdrop and dropped: the global claims file ends in January 2003, a casualty of that year's reporting-form change.

Regime trajectory
Weekly path through the growth/inflation plane. Regimes drift — they do not jump, which is why this framework is not a timing tool.
How the axes are built

Inflation comes entirely from Board 1 — the forward breakeven is the eliminator.

Growth blends the curve's level, collateral conditions and eurodollar quantity. The curve enters through Friedman's interest-rate fallacy as Snider applies it: a depressed 10-year yield means tight money and weak growth, not stimulus. That is the opposite of the conventional reading and it is deliberate.

Reading disagreement

Divergence is the point. When the signals split, that is information — not noise to be averaged away. Both 2008 and 2021–22 were disagreement periods, and in both the market signals were right and the aggregates were late.

When both axes sit near zero the board reports low conviction and promotes nothing to live. The framework earns its keep by ruling quadrants out, not by always naming a winner.
