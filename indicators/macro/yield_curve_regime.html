How it works

The four regimes

  • Rebound (bull steepening): yields fall, short end fastest. Rate cuts into a recession.
  • Calm (bear steepening): yields rise, long end fastest. A recovery being priced.
  • Speculation (bear flattening): yields rise, short end fastest. Policy tightening into a boom.
  • Turbulence (bull flattening): yields fall, long end fastest. Slower growth being priced.

The call

  • Weekly 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields from FRED, the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis database. Bull or bear = the sign of their average 13-week change. Steepening or flattening = the sign of the 13-week change in the gap between them.
  • The named regime changes only after a new regime shows 3 weeks in a row. The early read applies the same rule to 4-week moves, with no hold.
  • Conviction = the weaker of the two moves against a normal 13-week move over the past three years. Low, moderate and high are thirds of history.

The track record test

  • Each week's fund return is matched to the regime named the week before, so it only uses what was known at the time. Returns include dividends.
  • Assets use the fund's own return; industry groups use return relative to the S&P 500. Cyclicals = XLY, XLI, XLB. Defensives = XLP, XLU, XLV.
  • A cell is favor or avoid only if it differs from the rest of history by 1.5 standard errors and points the same way in both halves of history.
  • Limits: US curve only. Funds start 1998–2007 (Beta 2011, Bitcoin 2014), so each regime is a handful of episodes. No trading costs.
