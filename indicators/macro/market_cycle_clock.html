Where markets are pricing the economy, on two axes: growth and inflation. Eight price ratios (cyclicals vs staples, copper vs gold, inflation-protected vs regular Treasuries and so on) are each scored on their 13-week move, judged against their own typical swing. The signs of the two axes give the regime, using the same four quadrants as the Darius Dale page.

How to read it — and how it was tested

What it is for

  • A read of what markets are pricing for growth and inflation over the next few months. It is not a measure of where the economy is today.
  • When the markets and the real-economy ring sit in different quadrants, markets are pricing a turn the data hasn't shown yet, or the market is wrong. Watch which one moves.

How it was tested (2013–2026)

  • Each week's regime was compared with the economy's regime rebuilt month by month from federal economic data: payrolls, real income, industrial production, real sales and core inflation. These are the same inputs as the Darius Dale engine, and none of them are market prices.
  • It fits the economy best three months later. The named regime matched 32–36% of weeks against 21–25% by chance, across two versions of the economic reference. Since 2020 it matched about 40% against 22–24%. The same-month fit was somewhat weaker, and six months out it was mostly no better than chance.
  • Axis correlations with the economy three months later: growth about +0.3, inflation +0.3 to +0.45. 2013–2019 was weaker, mostly on the growth axis (+0.15).
  • The 3-week hold cut regime changes from 20% of weeks to 8%, with about the same accuracy: slightly better against one version of the reference, slightly worse against the other.
  • Not tested as a trading signal. Roughly 14 years covers only a few full cycles, so treat the margins as approximate.
