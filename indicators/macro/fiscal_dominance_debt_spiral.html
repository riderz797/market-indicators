COMPONENT Z-SCORES — WHAT IS DRIVING THE INDEX (click legend to isolate)
FORMULA
FDDS = 50 + 16.7 × Σ wi · zi
zi = 10-year rolling z-score of each input, clipped at ±3
w: Interest/GDP 30% · Core PCE 20% · Fed Assets 20% · Term Premium 15% · M2 15%

The debt burden (interest/GDP) carries the largest weight — it is the engine of the spiral. Inflation and balance-sheet growth measure whether the Fed is accommodating it. The term premium is the bond market's verdict on fiscal credibility, and M2 confirms whether accommodation is reaching the money supply.

SPIRAL CONDITION — RED MARKERS
z(Interest/GDP) > 0.5  AND  Core PCE > 2.5%
AND ( z(Fed Assets) > 0  OR  z(M2) > 0 )
AND  Fed Funds − Core PCE < 1%

The textbook fiscal-dominance signature: debt service is historically heavy, inflation is above target, money is easier than its decade norm, and the real policy rate is pinned below +1% — the central bank is financing the Treasury, not fighting inflation. Flagged months: 1967–68 (LBJ guns-and-butter), 1970–72 (Burns easing into Nixon's reelection), 1974–78, mid-1980, and Apr 2022. It never fires during the Volcker disinflation or 1990–2021. Today three of four conditions hold — it trips again the month Fed assets or M2 growth move back above their 10-year norm.

HOW TO READ IT
0–25Fiscal Discipline — light debt service, anchored inflation, market-priced term premium.
25–50Stable Funding — deficits absorbed without monetary help. Normal regime.
50–75Fiscal Strain — interest costs crowding the budget; policy independence eroding.
75–100Debt Spiral Risk — rates feed deficits feed issuance feed rates. Historically resolved by inflation, financial repression, or QE.

Direction matters as much as level: a fast rise through 50 with red markers appearing is the actionable signal — long duration becomes dangerous, hard assets and inflation hedges historically outperform.
